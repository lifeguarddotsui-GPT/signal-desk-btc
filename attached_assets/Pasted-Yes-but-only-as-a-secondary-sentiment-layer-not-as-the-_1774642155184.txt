Yes — but only as a secondary sentiment layer, not as the main reason to enter trades.

The useful part of this idea is not “copy traders.” It is this:

track crowd positioning, recent flow, and venue imbalance, then only let your agents trade when that sentiment agrees with price structure or when crowding is extreme enough to justify a contrarian fade. Typus and ZO both expose the exact kind of market structure that makes this worthwhile: perps, long/short positioning, funding, recent prints, oracle-based pricing, and leverage. Typus explicitly states that funding adjusts with long/short imbalance, and that trades which improve open-interest balance get lower impact fees while trades that worsen imbalance pay more. ZO’s SDK/docs also expose core perp concepts like long/short positions, funding, liquidation, order types, and oracle-based execution.  ￼

So the concept is valid, but the best version is:

price trend + momentum + crowd imbalance + fee/funding pressure + tape confirmation

not

blindly follow recent buys/sells.

Why this can help win rate:
When open interest becomes heavily one-sided, funding and fee mechanics start working against the crowded side on perps venues. On Typus in particular, imbalance matters twice: through funding and through impact-fee logic that penalizes trades that worsen LP exposure imbalance. That means your agents should prefer trades that either:
	1.	align with real momentum and are not too crowded, or
	2.	fade a severely crowded side once price starts losing momentum.  ￼

Why this should not be your primary signal:
Recent trade history can be noisy, spoofable, fragmented, and late. A burst of green prints on the tape can be exit liquidity instead of strength. Also, long/short ratio by itself is not enough; derivatives are always matched, so what matters is the imbalance signal you can actually observe on a venue: funding, displayed OI skew, change in OI, liquidation behavior, and whether price is confirming. External derivatives analytics also treat OI, funding, and long/short ratios as context indicators rather than standalone entry signals.  ￼

Best implementation

Build a new module for Replit called:

SentimentFlowEngine

It should score each market every minute and feed the score into your existing signal engine.

1. Inputs to collect

For each market you trade, collect:
	•	mark price
	•	1m / 5m / 15m / 1h returns
	•	EMA trend state
	•	RSI
	•	MACD histogram slope
	•	volume spike ratio
	•	venue open interest
	•	venue long-vs-short displayed skew if available
	•	funding / net rate
	•	recent trades feed: aggressive buys vs sells, trade count, total notional, largest prints
	•	whether the proposed order helps or worsens venue imbalance
	•	spread / latency / execution health

Typus docs confirm that funding and fee logic are affected by long/short imbalance and LP exposure. ZO docs confirm support for long/short positions, funding, liquidation, and order types through the Sudo SDK stack.  ￼

2. Add two sentiment modes

Create two new internal flags:
	•	trend_follow_sentiment
	•	contrarian_sentiment

Use them like this:

Trend-follow sentiment long
	•	price above fast and medium EMAs
	•	OI rising
	•	recent aggressive buys > sells
	•	funding not too extreme
	•	no exhaustion candle
	•	crowd not absurdly one-sided

Contrarian short
	•	price stalls under resistance
	•	crowd is heavily long
	•	OI high or rising into failure
	•	funding strongly favors shorts being paid
	•	recent tape flips from buy-dominant to sell-dominant

Do the reverse for long fades.

3. Use a weighted score

Do not let sentiment dominate. Cap it at 25–30% of the total signal.

Recommended composite:
	•	Trend structure: 30%
	•	Momentum: 20%
	•	Volume/participation: 15%
	•	Sentiment/OI/funding/flow: 25%
	•	Execution quality / latency / fee drag: 10%

4. Build a crowding score

Use a 0–100 scale:

crowdingScore =
  35 * normalizedOiSkew +
  25 * normalizedFundingExtreme +
  20 * normalizedRecentTapeImbalance +
  20 * normalizedOiExpansion

Interpretation:
	•	0–35 = neutral
	•	36–65 = directional bias exists
	•	66–80 = crowded
	•	81–100 = extreme crowding, only trade with confirmation or prepare contrarian fade

5. Add regime rules

This is the part that will actually improve win rate.

A. Momentum continuation regime
Trade with trend only when:
	•	EMA alignment is clean
	•	OI is increasing
	•	funding is not yet extreme
	•	recent tape confirms direction
	•	no major rejection wick against direction

This means “flow is joining a real move.”

B. Exhaustion regime
Do not chase when:
	•	OI very high
	•	funding extreme
	•	recent trade history one-sided
	•	price stops expanding or forms repeated rejection candles

This means “crowd is late.”

C. Contrarian squeeze regime
Fade only when:
	•	crowding is extreme
	•	price breaks structure against the crowded side
	•	tape flips for at least 2–3 intervals
	•	first entry is small

6. Prefer venue-aware execution

Because Typus explicitly charges different effective trading costs depending on whether a trade improves or worsens imbalance, your execution engine should estimate whether a proposed trade is “fee-favorable” or “fee-hostile.” If hostile, reduce size or require a higher signal threshold.  ￼

That is a big edge you are probably not using enough.

Bottom line

Yes, this can improve win rate, but only if you use it to answer:
	•	Is the move getting stronger?
	•	Is the crowd too one-sided?
	•	Is OI expanding with price or against price?
	•	Is funding/net-rate now a tax on the crowded side?
	•	Does this trade help or worsen venue imbalance and fees?

That approach is much stronger than simply following visible trade history. Typus’s own fee and funding design makes imbalance-aware trading especially relevant there, and ZO’s perp architecture supports building the same kind of sentiment layer into your agent stack.  ￼
